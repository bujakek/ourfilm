A QR code for wedding photos gives every guest the same simple starting point.
They scan once, enter their name and open the wedding camera in their mobile
browser. No one needs to search an app store, join a group chat or remember to
send photos after the honeymoon.

With OurFilm, the QR code does not open a public upload folder. It opens a
private digital disposable camera with a limited roll for each guest.

## How to create a wedding photo QR code

1. Create an OurFilm event and set its end time.
2. Choose 5, 10, 16, 24 or 36 shots for each participant.
3. Choose an immediate or end-of-event photo reveal.
4. Download or copy the event QR code.
5. Put the same code on signs, table cards and digital messages.
6. Test it on both an iPhone and an Android phone before printing.

The QR code and the shareable link lead to the same event. Guests who receive a
digital invitation can tap the link; guests at the venue can scan the printed
code.

## What guests see after scanning

The guest enters a display name and joins the event. Their phone opens a simple
camera showing the remaining number of shots. Taking a photo uses one frame,
and there is no preview or retake.

Photos either appear immediately or stay hidden until the event ends, depending
on the host's choice. Guests do not create an account and are not asked to pay.

## Where to place the QR code

One large sign at the entrance is easy to miss. Repeat the code at moments when
people are seated or waiting:

- one card on each dinner table;
- a small sign at the bar;
- beside the guest book or seating plan;
- near any photo prompt or prop area;
- in the wedding group or digital itinerary before the event.

Use the same design and wording everywhere. Repetition helps guests recognise
that each code opens the same shared wedding film.

## What to write beside it

The best sign explains the action and the reward. For example:

> Scan for your camera. You have 24 shots — help us capture the moments we miss.

If photos are hidden during the event, say when they will appear:

> Your photos develop when the party ends.

Avoid a bare “Scan me” message. It gives no reason to open the camera and does
not tell guests what will happen next.

## Why a browser camera reduces friction

Every extra step loses participants. Requiring an app download, account,
password or invitation approval is especially costly at a wedding, where
guests are not there to configure software.

OurFilm keeps the guest flow to a QR code, a name and the camera. The host owns
the event; guests do not need OurFilm accounts.

## Test before you print

Open the real event URL on more than one phone and check:

- the QR code scans from the intended print size;
- the venue has usable mobile data or Wi-Fi in the relevant rooms;
- the camera permission request is understandable;
- the chosen shot count and reveal setting are correct;
- the printed code points to the final event, not a design preview or test URL.

Testing the entire path matters more than making the sign decorative. A perfect
card with an old QR code cannot be fixed once hundreds of copies are on the
tables.

## QR code, shared album or disposable camera?

| Method                      | Best when                                              | Main trade-off                                                       |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Group chat                  | A small group already uses the same app                | Photos are mixed with conversation and depend on people sending them |
| Shared cloud album          | Guests should upload existing photos                   | Accounts and permissions can add friction                            |
| Physical disposable cameras | Real film and the physical object matter most          | Cameras must be bought, collected and developed                      |
| OurFilm QR camera           | You want a shared capture experience with finite shots | It is photo-only and does not accept camera-roll uploads             |
