The best time to collect wedding photos from guests is during the wedding. Once
people travel home, the request becomes another message they intend to answer
later. A week passes, camera rolls fill up and many of the best candid moments
never reach the couple.

The solution is not a more persistent reminder. It is a clear collection method
that guests encounter while they are already taking photos.

## Choose the experience before choosing the tool

Start with one question: should guests upload any photo they already have, or
should they take photos through a shared wedding camera?

- Choose a shared cloud album when camera-roll uploads and familiar storage are
  the priority.
- Choose a group chat only for a small group that already uses the same service.
- Choose physical disposable cameras when genuine film is part of the design.
- Choose a digital disposable camera when you want finite shots, participation
  during the event and one shared reveal.

OurFilm belongs to the last category. It does not import existing camera-roll
photos; guests capture new photos inside the event camera.

## A six-step collection plan

### 1. Set up the event early enough to test it

Create the real event before printing any QR material. Choose when the camera
closes, how many shots each participant gets and whether the gallery appears
immediately or at event end.

Run through the guest flow on at least two phones. Check the join screen, camera
permission, capture and reveal behaviour rather than testing only whether the
QR code opens.

### 2. Use one link everywhere

Your printed QR code and digital event link should lead to the same place. Do
not create separate albums for different tables or wedding groups unless you
actually want separate final galleries.

One destination is easier to explain and easier to download after the event.

### 3. Explain why guests should participate

“Upload your photos” sounds like administration. Give the activity a role in
the wedding instead:

> Help us capture the parts of the day we cannot see.

If you use limited shots, include the number. If the photos are hidden, include
the reveal time. Guests are more likely to join when they understand the game.

### 4. Put the QR code where photos happen

Use several small placements instead of relying on one entrance sign. Dinner
tables, the bar, guest book and dance-floor edge all give people time and a
reason to scan.

Ask the MC, celebrant or a member of the wedding party to mention the camera
once. A spoken prompt often reaches guests who ignored the printed card.

### 5. Give people ideas, not instructions

A short prompt can create more varied photos:

- someone you met today;
- the best-dressed guest;
- a view from your table;
- the quietest moment of the evening;
- the dance move that deserves evidence.

Keep prompts optional. The most valuable guest photos are often the ones nobody
planned.

### 6. Download and back up the final gallery

After the event, review the gallery, hide anything that should not be shared and
download the complete ZIP. Keep another copy somewhere separate from the event
service.

High-resolution files are worth preserving even when social copies have
already been posted.

## How many shots should each guest get?

| Roll length | A practical fit                                   |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 5 shots     | A short test, dinner or tightly curated challenge |
| 10 shots    | A reception or smaller evening event              |
| 16 shots    | A balanced half-day wedding experience            |
| 24 shots    | A full wedding day for most guests                |
| 36 shots    | A long event or a group that loves taking photos  |

There is no universal correct number. A shorter roll makes each frame more
deliberate; a longer one gives enthusiastic guests room without making the
camera unlimited.

## Common reasons guest photo collection fails

### The request comes too late

A reminder after the honeymoon competes with work, travel and hundreds of other
photos. Introduce the collection method before or during the event.

### The instructions require an account

Some guests will complete a sign-up flow. Many will not. Use the fewest steps
that still protect the event and identify each participant.

### The QR code appears only once

Guests entering a venue are greeting people, finding seats and putting away
coats. Repeat the code later, when they are ready to use it.

### Nobody knows when photos will appear

If the gallery is delayed, say so beside the QR code. Otherwise guests may
assume their photo failed or the page is broken.

### The method does not match the goal

A digital disposable camera is deliberately bad at collecting old camera-roll
photos. A shared upload folder is deliberately bad at creating a finite,
no-retake camera experience. Decide which outcome matters before comparing
feature lists.

## The simplest OurFilm setup

For a typical full-day wedding, start with 24 shots per guest and an
end-of-event reveal. Put the same [wedding photo QR
code](/en/qr-code-for-wedding-photos) on each table and ask someone to mention
it before dinner or speeches.

That is a starting point, not a rule. The best configuration is the one guests
can understand in one sentence.
