A wedding photo QR code works when it opens one tested guest destination and
explains what happens next. The code itself is only a shortcut; the camera or
upload experience behind it determines participation.

## 1. Choose the guest action

Decide whether guests should upload existing files or take new photos through a
shared camera. For a camera, choose the number of frames, when capture ends and
when the album reveals.

## 2. Create the real event first

Do not print a temporary code. Finish the event setup, copy the final URL and
generate the QR from that address. Printed materials are expensive to replace.

## 3. Test the full journey

Use at least one iPhone and one Android phone. Test the scan, landing page,
camera permission, capture, upload and gallery state. Repeat with mobile data
and venue Wi-Fi if possible.

## 4. Design for scanning

Use dark code modules on a light quiet background. Leave clear space around the
code, avoid glossy glare and print a readable fallback URL. Test the smallest
card from the distance guests will actually use.

## 5. Repeat it where photos happen

Place small copies on tables, near the bar, guestbook and dance-floor edge. One
large entrance sign is easy to miss while guests are arriving.

## 6. Announce it once

Ask the MC or wedding party to say:

> Scan the code on your table, open our shared camera and help us capture the
> moments we cannot see. Your photos will appear after the event.
