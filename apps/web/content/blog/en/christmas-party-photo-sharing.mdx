Christmas party photo sharing works best when the organiser gives colleagues one
clear destination before the first toast. Decide whether guests should upload
pictures already on their phones or take new ones with a shared guest camera,
then put the same tested QR code wherever people naturally pause.

A work party needs more than festive signage. Someone must own the gallery,
explain who can see it and decide whether any picture may appear on the company
intranet or social channels. A simple plan protects colleagues without turning
the evening into another policy presentation.

## Choose the photo experience before inviting the team

### Use an upload album for pictures already taken

An upload album lets colleagues use their normal camera apps and choose what to
contribute afterwards. It suits formal portraits, pictures from several office
locations and existing team memories collected before the party. Check whether
the service accepts video, requires an account, preserves the quality you need
and lets an organiser remove an accidental upload.

Write “upload your photos” beside this kind of QR code. Guests should know
whether they are choosing files from their phones, whether other colleagues can
see them and how long contributions remain open.

### Use a guest camera for new pictures during the party

A digital guest camera turns photography into part of the evening. Each person
gets a finite roll, so the team looks for moments worth keeping instead of
adding every near-duplicate from a camera roll.

OurFilm follows this model. A colleague scans the QR code, opens the event in a
phone browser, enters a name and receives the roll chosen by the host: 5, 10,
16, 24 or 36 frames. No app or guest account is required. There is no preview
or retake, and the private gallery can reveal immediately or when the event
ends.

If the host enables the optional after-event window, guests can add saved photos
for 24 hours after the event ends, using frames left on their original rolls.
OurFilm is not an unlimited camera-roll uploader and it does not accept video.
State that boundary before somebody expects to send every clip from the dance
floor on Monday.

## Give the gallery an owner and an audience

Assign one organiser before printing the QR code. That person should know the
event settings, hold the final download and respond if a colleague wants a
picture hidden. A second organiser can help during a large party, but avoid a
shared password or an unowned link in a team channel.

Agree on four things with HR, internal communications or the event lead:

- who may join the camera;
- whether guests may open the finished gallery;
- whether the collection is only a private keepsake or may feed company posts;
- who approves any picture before it is used beyond the invited group.

Taking part in a private party gallery is not blanket consent for marketing.
If the company wants photographs for recruitment, press or public social media,
run that permission process separately and make declining easy.

Contractors, partners and plus-ones need the same clarity as employees. Do not
assume they have read an internal policy or can access the company intranet the
next morning.

## Build the camera into the run of show

### Arrival: introduce it after coats and name badges

Put the first code after the check-in point, not inside the arrival queue.
Colleagues need free hands to remove coats, collect a drink and greet people.
A welcome table or standing card beside the first refreshment stop gives them
time to scan.

Use the brighter first part of the evening for department pictures and the
official full-team photograph. A professional photographer remains the safer
choice when the company needs a guaranteed, carefully lit group portrait. The
guest camera captures everything happening around it.

### Dinner and speeches: ask for reactions, not screens

Make one short announcement after most people are seated. Ask for reactions to
the speech, details on the tables and colleagues meeting outside their usual
teams. During an award presentation, one assigned photographer should secure
the handover picture while guests capture the audience and congratulations.

Keep phones down when confidential results, unreleased products or private
business information appear on a screen. If photography is restricted during a
part of the programme, say exactly when it pauses and when it can resume.

### Secret Santa and games: protect the surprise

If the party includes Secret Santa, tell participants not to reveal gifts or
names in a live gallery before the exchange is complete. A gallery that opens
only when the event ends can preserve the surprise and stop guests reviewing
every frame between rounds.

Optional photo prompts work well around a quiz, ugly-jumper contest or table
game, but nobody should have to perform for a camera. Object pictures—wrapped
gifts, decorations, food or a team-made prop—let camera-shy colleagues join
without appearing in a portrait.

### Dance floor and late night: shorten the instructions

By the time the music gets louder, nobody will read a paragraph. Repeat only the
action: scan, enter a name and take a picture. Keep QR cards away from wet bars,
speaker stacks and the edge of the dance floor.

Do not create prompts that encourage drinking, climbing on furniture or
photographing someone who is asleep, unwell or unable to consent. The gallery
should remember the team at its best, not reward the most intrusive picture.

### The next workday: review before sharing

When the gallery opens, let the organiser handle removal requests and download
the full collection. Share the private link only with the agreed audience.
Select a separate, approved set for the intranet or public channels rather than
copying the whole gallery into company communications.

Keep a second backup after downloading. If the after-event window is enabled,
send one concise reminder with its real deadline; do not chase colleagues in
several chats for the same files.

## Put the QR code where colleagues already pause

Use the same final code in several practical places:

- beside check-in, after badges have been collected;
- on upright table cards or menus;
- near the buffet or hot-drink station, away from spills;
- beside the gift or Secret Santa table;
- at the edge of a photo corner;
- on a static slide before the formal programme begins;
- in the private invitation or team message.

Print dark modules on a light, matte background with clear space around the
square. Metallic Christmas stock and glossy lamination can reflect venue lights
across the code. Add a short typed link underneath, then test the printed card
on iPhone and Android using both venue Wi-Fi and mobile data.

If several offices attend, decide whether they should share one event or keep
separate galleries. One camera is useful when everyone belongs to the same
celebration and may see the same collection. Separate cameras make more sense
when access rules or organisers differ.

## Use wording that makes the action obvious

For a conventional upload album:

> Add your favourite Christmas party photos. Scan the code, choose the best
> pictures from tonight and upload them to our private team album. Please check
> with colleagues before sharing their portraits elsewhere.

For an OurFilm guest camera:

> Photograph the Christmas party through your eyes. Scan, enter your name and
> use your roll tonight. No app or account. Our private gallery opens when the
> party ends.

For a screen before dinner:

> One party, every viewpoint. Scan now so the camera is ready later. Taking
> part is optional, and the gallery stays with the invited team.

Replace “private” if the selected setup is intended for a wider audience. Do
not promise a live slideshow, video uploads, filters or unlimited photos unless
the tool you have actually tested provides them.

## Give the team prompts that cross department lines

Use a short optional list rather than a competitive hunt:

- a colleague you usually see only on a video call;
- the best handmade decoration;
- two departments in one frame;
- the reaction to an award or speech;
- the most creative festive outfit;
- a gift before and after it is opened;
- the view from your table;
- the quietest moment of the evening;
- the first and last group on the dance floor;
- the people who made the event happen.

Avoid “funniest drunk colleague”, kissing challenges or prompts about seniority,
age, religion or family status. A work event still has workplace boundaries,
even after office hours.

## Treat workplace privacy as part of the setup

A QR code is a visual link, not a password. Anyone who receives or photographs
it may pass it on. Keep it inside the invited group, avoid displaying it in a
public event listing and explain who can view the gallery.

Ask before photographing close portraits, and provide a named removal contact.
Watch backgrounds for visible badges, visitor lists, presentation slides,
laptop screens and whiteboards. Do not publish a colleague's image simply
because they appeared in a private album.

## Christmas party photo-sharing checklist

- [ ] The organiser has chosen upload or new-photo capture.
- [ ] One person owns access, moderation, download and backup.
- [ ] The gallery audience and public-use rules are written down.
- [ ] The final printed QR code works on real phones at the venue.
- [ ] Photography pauses are included in the run of show.
- [ ] Guests know the reveal timing and removal contact.
- [ ] Prompts remain optional, inclusive and safe around alcohol.
- [ ] Public company posts use a separately approved selection.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to collect Christmas party photos?

Give the team one tested QR code and one precise instruction. Use an upload
album for pictures colleagues take with their normal camera apps, or a
browser-based guest camera for new pictures made during the party. Assign an
organiser to moderate, download and back up the collection.

### Where should the Christmas party photo QR code go?

Place the same code after check-in, on tables and near the buffet, gift table or
photo corner. Keep it upright, dry and well lit. Include a short fallback link
and test the printed version with the venue connection before guests arrive.

### Should the gallery open during or after the party?

An end-of-event reveal keeps Secret Santa, awards and candid pictures from
appearing early and lets colleagues stay present. An instant gallery can suit a
deliberately moderated display. State the choice beside the QR code.

### Do colleagues need an app to use OurFilm?

No. They open the event in a phone browser, enter a name and use the finite roll
chosen by the host. There is no account, preview or retake.

### Can colleagues add saved photos the next day?

Only if the host enables OurFilm's optional after-event window. It lasts for 24
hours after the event ends and uses frames left on each person's original roll.
It is not unlimited, and video is not accepted.
