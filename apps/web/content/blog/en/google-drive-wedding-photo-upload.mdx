**In short:** Google Drive can store wedding guest photos in their original file
form, but a normal shared folder is not a frictionless public upload form.
Contributors may need a Google account, and broad edit permission can expose
other guests' files.

## Configure permissions deliberately

Create a dedicated wedding folder rather than sharing a personal Drive
directory. Test the contribution flow with an account that is not the owner.
Confirm exactly what that person can upload, rename, move or delete.

Avoid publishing an editable link before this test. “Anyone with the link” is
not the same as “anyone may upload without signing in,” and Drive behaviour can
vary by account type and sharing policy.

If many unrelated guests must upload, use a front-end form or wedding upload
service that writes into Drive rather than exposing the folder itself.

## Estimate storage and organise the files

Leave enough quota for photos and videos, then use a simple structure:

- `01-original-guest-uploads`
- `02-professional-photos`
- `03-selected-favourites`
- `04-album-and-print-files`

Never edit the only copy of an original. Copy selected files into a working
folder and keep a second backup outside the Google account.

## When Drive is the right choice

Drive works well for a small known group, a couple already committed to Google
storage, or a workflow where long-term file ownership matters more than the
guest interface.

It is less effective as an activity during the reception. Uploading existing
files feels administrative, and guests may not understand which permission or
account prompt they are seeing.

For a lower-friction route, compare a [QR-code wedding photo
album](/en/qr-code-for-wedding-photos) with a service that sends uploads into
your own Drive.
