**In short:** wedding entertainment works when it supports the rhythm of the day
instead of competing with it. Use self-directed activities during waiting
periods, focused moments between courses and music-led energy after formal
events finish.

## Before dinner

A live acoustic set, lawn games, local tasting station, guest camera or photo
scavenger hunt gives early arrivals something to do without demanding a fixed
start time.

## During dinner

Keep activities short. Table conversation cards, a couple quiz, one speech
block or a visual slideshow can work. Avoid repeated interruptions that prevent
guests from eating and talking.

## After dinner

A band or DJ remains the central engine for many receptions. Add one surprise
such as a short performance, cultural tradition, late-night food reveal or
collective song rather than stacking a full stage programme.

## For guests who prefer quieter spaces

Provide a lounge away from speakers, simple table games, a message wall and a
well-lit route outside. Entertainment is also the ability to have a
conversation comfortably.

## For children

Use age-appropriate crafts, puzzles, soft play or a supervised film area.
Avoid small props, unsecured equipment and public photo activities without
guardian agreement.

## Photo entertainment

A booth creates a destination. A [digital wedding guest
camera](/en/digital-disposable-camera-for-weddings) distributes the activity
throughout the venue. Choose one main photo instruction and make it optional.
