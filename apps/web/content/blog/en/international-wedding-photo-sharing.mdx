For an international guest list, choose a photo tool whose guest page is easy
to understand, provide short instructions in the two most useful languages and
offer venue Wi-Fi. The guest-facing language matters more than the host
dashboard language.

## Choose shared languages

Do not create a separate translation for every passport. Identify one or two
languages most guests can use. Ask native speakers to review the final copy,
especially verbs such as scan, join, take, upload and reveal.

## Keep the sign literal

Avoid wordplay that disappears in translation. A clear bilingual example is:

> Share your photos / [second-language translation]
>
> Scan the code. No app or account required. The album opens after the party.

Only make claims the selected service actually supports.

## Test the guest page

Ask an international friend to complete the flow without instructions. Test
date and time formats, browser language, camera permission and error messages.
A translated sign cannot repair an incomprehensible upload page.

## Plan connectivity

Some guests will avoid roaming or have no local data. Display the guest Wi-Fi
name and password separately, and provide a clickable link in the wedding
website or message for people who cannot scan.

## Allow a follow-up window

Upload-first galleries should remain open long enough for travellers to
contribute from stable internet after returning home. Capture-only cameras need
participation during the event, so communicate that deadline clearly.

Compare multilingual and access requirements when choosing a
[wedding photo-sharing app](/en/wedding-photo-sharing-app).
