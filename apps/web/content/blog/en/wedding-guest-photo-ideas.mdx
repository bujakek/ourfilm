The best wedding guest photos show moments the couple and professional team
cannot see: table conversations, reactions before the entrance, old friends
reconnecting and the final quiet minutes after the dance floor empties.

## Getting ready

- coffee, breakfast and unfinished details;
- hands helping with buttons, ties or jewellery;
- the room before everyone is ready;
- friends seeing the finished outfit for the first time.

## Ceremony

Follow the venue's photography rules. Useful guest perspectives include the
processional from a seat, reactions in the rows, hands held during readings and
the crowd immediately after the ceremony.

## Dinner and speeches

Photograph the speaker and the people reacting. Capture the view from each
table, shared dishes, place cards, children drawing and relatives telling
stories between formal moments.

## Dance floor

Move closer instead of using extreme zoom. Look for groups forming, shoes left
at the edge, the first brave dancers and the expressions around one dramatic
move. Direct flash can suit the energy if it does not distract performers or
the professional photographer.

## Late night

The final snack, staff resetting tables, tired friends, an empty venue corner
and the last goodbye often become the most personal photographs.

Give guests ideas without assigning them a shot list. A
[finite wedding guest camera](/en/digital-disposable-camera-for-weddings) makes
each choice more deliberate.
