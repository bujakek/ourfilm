Prom photo sharing works best when the organising committee gives everyone one
clear place for pictures before the doors open. A QR code can lead to a shared
upload album or a browser-based guest camera, but those are different
experiences: one collects photos already on students' phones, while the other
invites them to take new pictures during the event.

Choose that experience first. Then agree who owns the album, where phones are
welcome and when the gallery will become visible. A thoughtful setup captures
arrivals, friendship groups and dance-floor moments without competing with the
official photographer or turning a private school event into a public feed.

## Choose the prom photo experience before the platform

### Use an upload album for pictures already on guests' phones

A shared upload album is useful when students will photograph with their usual
camera apps and contribute their favourites later. It can gather polished
group portraits, preparation photos and pictures from different friendship
groups that would otherwise remain scattered across chats.

Before choosing a service, check whether contributors need an app or account,
whether they can remove an accidental upload and when submissions close. Also
ask whether every guest can browse the complete gallery or only the organiser
can review it. Those details matter at a school event where many attendees may
be under 18.

### Use a guest camera to create new candid pictures

A digital guest camera opens a camera after the scan and makes photography
part of the night. A finite roll encourages students to notice a few moments
that matter instead of sending every duplicate from their camera roll.

OurFilm follows this model. Guests open the event in a browser, enter a name
and receive the roll chosen by the host: 5, 10, 16, 24 or 36 frames. There is
no preview or retake. The host decides whether the private gallery reveals
immediately, when the event ends or at a later time.

If the host enables the optional after-event window, guests can add saved
photos for 24 hours after the event ends, using frames left on their original
rolls. OurFilm is not an unlimited camera-roll uploader and it does not accept
video.

### Keep the official photographer focused on the essential shots

A professional photographer remains the reliable choice for formal arrivals,
couples, year-group portraits and carefully lit pictures. Guest photos solve a
different problem: they preserve reactions, table conversations and the view
from inside each friendship group.

If you use both, publish a simple rule. For example, ask guests to leave the
entrance portrait to the photographer, then invite candid pictures once they
reach their tables. The two collections will complement one another instead
of producing a wall of phones around every formal moment.

## Give the album a real owner

Prom planning is shared, but a photo album still needs one accountable owner.
Choose a student committee member, staff adviser or parent representative who
will:

- create the final event and keep the organiser access;
- confirm the school and venue rules;
- test the guest flow before anything is printed;
- answer questions during the event;
- review, download and back up the gallery afterwards.

Name a second person who can step in during arrivals or dinner. Do not make the
DJ, photographer or venue coordinator responsible unless that work is
explicitly part of their agreement.

One shared collection is simplest for a single prom. Split it only when there
is a genuine access reason—for example, separate school groups cannot share a
gallery, or an after-party has a different guest list. Several nearly identical
QR codes on the same table create confusion and fragment the story.

## Confirm school, venue and safeguarding rules

Before printing the QR code, ask the school and venue:

- whether students may use phones during dinner, awards and speeches;
- where photography is restricted;
- whether QR signs can be placed at entrances or tables;
- whether any students or staff must not appear in shared pictures;
- who can approve the gallery and handle a removal request;
- whether the venue Wi-Fi allows the camera or album page to open.

A QR code is only a visual link. Anyone who photographs or receives it may be
able to pass it on, so do not display a private prom link on a public noticeboard
or unrestricted social post. Keep it inside the invited group and use the
service's access controls.

Photography permission is not the same as permission to publish. A picture in
a private event gallery should not be posted publicly or used by a school,
venue or supplier without the appropriate consent. Follow the school's own
safeguarding and media policies, especially when attendees are minors.

## Set up prom photo sharing step by step

1. **Choose capture or upload.** Decide whether guests should take new photos,
   contribute existing ones or use a clearly limited combination.
2. **Create the final event.** Set the event name, opening and closing times,
   access and reveal timing before generating material for print.
3. **Test as a guest.** Scan from an iPhone and Android device, grant the needed
   permission and complete one contribution without the host login.
4. **Test at the venue.** Try both guest Wi-Fi and mobile data in the ballroom,
   dining area and any lower-ground or interior spaces.
5. **Print one final QR code.** Add a short typed link as a fallback for a
   damaged sign or a phone that struggles to scan.
6. **Brief the helpers.** Give the photographer, host and staff adviser the
   same one-sentence explanation so guests receive a consistent answer.

Test the actual printed card, not only the file on a laptop. Metallic paper,
lamination, coloured lighting and a code printed too small can all make an
otherwise valid link difficult to scan.

## Put the QR code at natural pauses

The entrance creates awareness, but arrivals are busy. Repeat the same code
where guests have time and free hands:

- on table cards, away from drinks and reflective decorations;
- beside the seating plan or coat check after the initial queue;
- near a staffed photo backdrop;
- on a screen between scheduled announcements, if the school approves;
- in the private event message before prom and once again the next morning.

Do not place signs in emergency routes, on a crowded dance-floor edge or where
they could be confused with venue instructions. A few well-timed placements
work better than a different sign on every surface.

## Write an instruction that explains the action

“Scan for prom photos” does not tell a guest whether to view, upload or take a
picture. State the action, app requirement and reveal time in one short block.

For an upload album:

> Add your prom pictures to our private shared album. Scan, choose your
> favourites and submit them by noon tomorrow. No public posting without the
> people pictured agreeing.

For a guest camera:

> Show us prom from your table and the dance floor. Scan, enter your name and
> use your 16 frames tonight. No app or account. The private gallery reveals
> tomorrow at 10 a.m.

Ask the host or committee to make one reminder after everyone is seated. If
the room has a screen, a brief approved slide during a natural break can help.
Avoid repeated announcements during speeches or the final dance.

## Use prompts that cover more than the dance floor

Give guests a short optional list rather than a rigid scavenger hunt:

- the arrival look before coats and bags disappear;
- shoes, corsages, boutonnieres and small details;
- every person at your table in one frame;
- the friend who made you laugh during dinner;
- a reaction to the first song;
- the organising team finally enjoying the night;
- a quiet moment away from the dance floor;
- the last friendship-group picture before leaving.

Do not turn staff, chaperones or other students into compulsory photo subjects.
Avoid prompts that encourage risky poses, standing on furniture or entering a
restricted backstage area. The aim is to notice the night, not disrupt it.

## Decide when everyone should see the gallery

An immediate gallery suits a moderated live display, but it can pull attention
towards screens and requires someone to manage what appears. A reveal after
prom keeps the pictures a surprise and gives the organiser time to respond to
anything inappropriate before broader sharing.

Whatever you choose, state it next to the code. Students should know whether
their picture can appear during the event, after the final song or the next
morning. Do not promise a live slideshow when the chosen service only offers a
later gallery.

## Review and close the collection responsibly

After prom, the album owner should:

1. respond to hiding or deletion requests;
2. review frames for readable tickets, name badges or location details;
3. download the final collection and keep a separate backup;
4. share it only with the agreed audience;
5. close contribution access when it is no longer needed.

Do not assume every student wants every picture shared on social media. Ask
before posting close portraits, awkward moments or photos of someone who has
already left the event. A smaller private gallery can be more valuable than a
public album nobody feels safe contributing to.

## Prom photo-sharing checklist

- [ ] The committee chose an upload album or guest camera deliberately.
- [ ] One owner and one backup helper are named.
- [ ] School, venue and safeguarding rules are confirmed.
- [ ] The final link works on iPhone, Android, Wi-Fi and mobile data.
- [ ] The printed code scans in the actual lighting.
- [ ] Signs explain the action, app requirement and reveal time.
- [ ] Students know who can view the gallery and request removal.
- [ ] Download, backup and closure have an owner.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to collect prom photos from everyone?

Give every invited guest the same tested link or QR code. Use an upload album
for pictures already on their phones, or a guest camera for new pictures taken
during prom. Explain the action beside the code and make one person responsible
for the finished gallery.

### Do students need an app to share prom photos?

That depends on the service. OurFilm opens in a phone browser, so guests do not
need an app or account. Test the exact flow on iPhone and Android before
printing and do not claim “no app” for a platform unless it is true.

### Where should a prom photo QR code go?

Use the same code on table cards, near the seating plan or staffed photo area,
and in a private event message. Keep it away from queues, dance-floor edges and
public school channels. Include a short typed link as a fallback.

### Can guests upload existing prom photos to OurFilm?

Only when the host enables the optional after-event window. For 24 hours after
the event ends, guests may use frames remaining on their original rolls to add
saved photos. OurFilm does not accept video or provide unlimited uploads.

### Should the prom gallery reveal during or after the event?

An instant reveal suits a moderated live experience. A later reveal keeps
attention on prom and creates a shared surprise afterwards. Choose before the
event and state the timing clearly on every sign.
