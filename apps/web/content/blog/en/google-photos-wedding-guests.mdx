**In short:** a Google Photos shared album is familiar, visual and easy to keep
after a wedding. It can work well when most guests already use Google Photos.
The main risk is assuming that every phone will join and contribute with the
same number of steps.

## Test the real guest flow

Create the album, enable collaboration only if guests should add media, then
open the share link on:

- an Android phone signed into Google;
- an iPhone with Google Photos installed;
- an iPhone without the app;
- a signed-out private browser.

Record which flows require an account or app and what guests can see. A QR code
only shortens the link; it does not remove the service's sign-in requirements.

## Understand storage and quality

Shared albums are convenient for viewing, but long-term ownership still needs a
download and backup plan. After the wedding, download representative photos and
check their pixel dimensions, file type and metadata before relying on them for
prints.

The couple should also decide who may add files, whether guests may invite
others and when collaboration will close. A link copied outside the wedding
group can widen access.

## Where Google Photos fits

Choose it when the goal is a familiar shared album that people can browse and
continue updating. It is less suitable when you need anonymous uploads, a
strictly private unguessable event, a per-person shot limit or a delayed first
reveal.

If those event controls matter, compare it with a dedicated
[wedding photo-sharing app](/en/wedding-photo-sharing-app).
