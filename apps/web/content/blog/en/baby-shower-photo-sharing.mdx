Baby shower photo sharing is simplest when every guest receives one tested link
or QR code. Before printing it, decide what should happen after the scan: should
people upload pictures already stored on their phones, or should a shared guest
camera invite them to take new photos during the shower?

That choice matters more than the QR code itself. An upload album is useful for
collecting existing pictures. A guest camera turns photography into part of the
celebration and captures reactions from across the room. Either can work, but
the sign, timing and finished album will be different.

## Choose between an upload album and a guest camera

### Use an upload album for pictures already taken

A shared upload album lets guests photograph with their usual camera app and
select their favourites later. It is useful when you want several versions of
a group portrait, pictures from preparations or contributions from someone who
could not attend.

Check whether guests need an app or account, whether they can remove an
accidental upload and how long contributions remain open. Also decide whether
everyone can browse the gallery or only the organiser can review it. Those
details affect privacy as much as the word “private” on a sales page.

### Use a guest camera for new moments at the shower

A browser-based guest camera asks people to take new pictures after they scan.
It suits a shower where the photo activity itself should be playful: each guest
notices different decorations, conversations and reactions rather than sorting
through a camera roll after going home.

OurFilm follows this model. A guest opens the event in a browser, enters a name
and receives the roll selected by the host: 5, 10, 16, 24 or 36 frames. There is
no preview or retake, and the host decides whether the private gallery reveals
immediately, at the end of the event or later.

If the host enables the optional after-event window, guests can add saved
photos for 24 hours after the event ends, using any frames left on their
original rolls. This is not an unlimited camera-roll upload service, and it
does not accept video.

## Decide who owns the setup and the finished album

The person organising a baby shower is not always the parent-to-be. A sibling,
friend or coworker may arrange the invitations, decorate the room and manage
the guest list. Agree on three things before creating the album:

1. **Who creates and tests it?** That person needs time before anything goes to
   print.
2. **Who can review the photos?** The organiser may manage the activity, while
   the parent-to-be should decide what can be shared more widely.
3. **Who receives the archive?** Make the download and backup part of the gift,
   not an unresolved job after the party.

If the shower is a surprise, keep the event link out of messages or documents
the guest of honour might see. Introduce the QR code only after the surprise is
safe.

## Set up the baby shower QR code step by step

1. **Choose capture or upload.** Write the guest action in one sentence before
   comparing services.
2. **Create the real event.** Set the event name, access, capture window and
   reveal behaviour before generating the final code.
3. **Test as a guest.** Scan on an iPhone and Android phone, follow every
   permission step and complete one genuine contribution without the host
   account.
4. **Check the venue connection.** Try both guest Wi-Fi and mobile data in the
   room, garden or café where people will use it.
5. **Print one physical sample.** Test the actual size and paper finish in the
   event lighting. A glossy card can reflect enough light to make scanning
   awkward.
6. **Keep a short URL beside the code.** It provides a fallback for a damaged
   sign or a guest whose camera does not recognise the QR code.

Do this before invitations, favour tags or welcome signs go to print. A code
can scan perfectly and still lead to a draft event or the organiser's private
management page.

## Put the code where the moments happen

The entrance introduces the idea, but guests rarely stop there long enough to
participate. Repeat the same code at two or three natural pauses:

- beside the guest book, advice cards or prediction cards;
- near the drinks or dessert table;
- by the gift-opening area, but not where it will appear in every formal photo;
- on tables where guests sit during games;
- on a small card inside a party favour;
- in the private invitation or group message as a backup link.

Avoid making a new album for every table or activity. One destination is easier
to explain and gives the parents-to-be one coherent collection afterwards.

## Write instructions guests can act on

“Scan me” explains the mechanism, not the reason. A useful sign states the
action, whether an app is needed and when the gallery will open.

For a shared upload album:

> Add your baby shower photos. Scan the code, choose your favourites and send
> them to our private album. No group chat needed.

For a guest camera:

> Show the shower from your point of view. Scan the code, enter your name and
> use your 16 frames today. No app or account. We will reveal the album
> tomorrow.

Ask the host to mention it once before a photo-friendly moment such as games or
gift opening. One clear announcement works better than repeatedly interrupting
the room.

## Give guests baby shower photo prompts

Prompts help when friends, relatives and coworkers do not all know one another.
Keep them optional and focused on moments that can happen naturally:

- the parent-to-be arriving;
- the smallest detail in the decorations;
- two guests meeting for the first time;
- a handwritten advice or wishes card;
- a grandparent, aunt, uncle or chosen family member reacting;
- the funniest moment during a game;
- the gift wrap before it disappears;
- one picture of the organiser who is usually behind the camera;
- the view from your seat;
- everyone who helped set up;
- a quiet conversation away from the main activity;
- the last group still talking after dessert.

Do not turn every prompt into a compulsory pose. The value of a guest album is
that people notice different things. A short list starts the activity without
making the shower feel like a photo shoot.

## Handle reveals and surprises carefully

Not every baby shower includes a gender reveal, pregnancy announcement or
public social-media post. Do not build the instructions around one unless the
parents-to-be have explicitly chosen it.

If a reveal is part of the event, decide whether guest photos may appear during
the party or only afterwards. A delayed gallery can protect the surprise and
stop a screen in the room from showing a revealing picture too early. Ask
guests not to post publicly before the family has shared the news themselves.

The same applies to an unannounced pregnancy. Keep the QR code inside the
invited group and avoid putting it on a publicly visible venue board.

## Protect the family's privacy

A QR code is a visual link, not a password. Anyone who receives or photographs
it may be able to pass it on. Review the service's access controls, keep the
code within the guest list and state who can view or download the finished
gallery.

Baby shower pictures may reveal an ultrasound image, full name, due date, home
address on a label or personal information on a card. Check the frame before
taking the picture, and do not make private documents part of a prompt.

Give guests a named contact for hiding or removing a photo. Ask before posting
someone else's portrait publicly, especially when children appear. Contributing
to a private album is not automatic permission for social-media or promotional
use.

After the shower, review the collection, download it, make a separate backup
and close contribution access when it is no longer needed.

## Baby shower photo-sharing checklist

- [ ] The guest action is clearly capture, upload or a defined combination.
- [ ] The organiser and album recipient have agreed on access.
- [ ] The final link works on iPhone, Android, Wi-Fi and mobile data.
- [ ] The sign states the action, app requirement and reveal timing.
- [ ] The same code appears at two or three natural activity points.
- [ ] A host will mention it once before a good photo moment.
- [ ] Surprise and public-posting expectations are clear.
- [ ] Review, download, backup and closure have an owner.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I collect baby shower photos from guests?

Create one shared upload album or guest camera, connect its final link to a QR
code and test the whole guest journey. Display the same code at a few natural
stopping points and explain whether guests should upload existing pictures or
take new ones.

### Do baby shower guests need to download an app?

Not always. OurFilm opens in a phone browser, so guests do not need an app or
account. Check the exact service you choose before printing and promise “no
app” only if the tested guest flow confirms it.

### Can guests add pictures already on their phones to OurFilm?

Only when the host enables the optional after-event window. For 24 hours after
the event ends, guests can add saved photos using frames remaining on their
original rolls. Video and unlimited uploads are not supported.

### Where should I put a baby shower photo QR code?

Place it near the guest book or advice cards, then repeat the same code by the
dessert table, games or gift-opening area. Choose places where guests have a
free hand, enough light and time to scan.

### Is a baby shower QR album private?

It can be restricted to invited guests, but the QR code itself is not a
password. Keep it off public pages, review access settings and tell guests who
will be able to see or download the pictures.
