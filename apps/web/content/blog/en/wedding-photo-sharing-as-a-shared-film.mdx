**In short:** wedding photo sharing does not have to begin with an upload
request. A shared digital film gives guests one camera during the event, a
finite number of frames and one reveal. The couple receives candid viewpoints
without chasing dozens of folders afterwards.

## Make the collection method part of the day

Introduce the camera in one sentence: scan, enter a name and help photograph
the parts of the wedding the couple cannot see. Put the same QR code on tables,
near the bar and beside the guestbook.

Avoid multiple competing albums. One destination is easier to explain, easier
to moderate and easier to archive.

## Use a finite roll deliberately

Five frames create a small challenge. Ten or sixteen suit a shorter reception.
Twenty-four gives enthusiastic guests enough room for a full wedding day. The
limit should guide participation, not punish someone who joins late.

## Choose the reveal

An immediate gallery creates conversation and can motivate more participation.
An end-of-event reveal keeps attention on taking photos and creates a shared
surprise. State the choice beside the QR code so guests do not mistake a hidden
album for a failed upload.

## Preserve the result

After the wedding, hide anything sensitive, download the complete gallery and
make an independent backup. The online service should not be the only copy of a
once-only event.
