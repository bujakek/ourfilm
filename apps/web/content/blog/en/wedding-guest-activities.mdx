Good wedding guest activities are optional, easy to understand and available
during natural gaps. They should help people connect without forcing shy guests
to perform or interrupting the ceremony, meal and dancing.

## Fifteen practical ideas

1. A QR-code digital guest camera
2. A ten-prompt photo scavenger hunt
3. An audio guestbook phone or web recorder
4. A written advice wall
5. A couple quiz at each table
6. Conversation cards for mixed groups
7. A Polaroid or Instax guestbook
8. A simple board-game table
9. A children's craft and puzzle corner
10. A collaborative playlist request board
11. A map where guests mark where they travelled from
12. A memory card for stories about the couple
13. A dessert or local-food tasting station
14. A calm outdoor lounge
15. A shared album that reveals after the event

## Match activities to the schedule

Use quiet activities during arrival and dinner. Save collaborative games for a
clear transition. Keep the dance-floor period free from repeated instructions.

## Make participation inclusive

Provide seated and non-digital options, readable type, simple language and no
public scoring for personal messages. Children need a safe activity suited to
their age, not merely adult props.

## Choose fewer, better activities

Two well-placed options beat ten unattended stations. Assign someone to check
materials, lighting and technology without turning them into a full-time host.

A [shared wedding camera](/en/digital-disposable-camera-for-weddings) is useful
because guests can participate for seconds or throughout the event.
