**In short:** a wedding audio guestbook records spoken messages instead of, or
alongside, written notes. It can use a physical telephone at the venue or a
browser link that guests open by QR code. The best setup is quiet, obvious and
tested with the exact delivery format.

## Vintage telephone guestbook

A dedicated phone creates a visible activity and usually begins recording when
the handset is lifted. Ask the supplier about maximum message length, power,
backup recording, delivery format and whether raw files are included.

Place it away from speakers and add a small light. Guests need enough privacy to
speak without blocking a corridor.

## Web-based voice messages

A QR recorder reaches more guests and can include people who are not present.
It depends on browser microphone permission, network access and clear privacy
language. Test iPhone and Android flows before printing the code.

## Prompts that produce better messages

Use one or two optional questions:

- What should we remember about today?
- What is your favourite story about us?
- What advice should we hear on our first anniversary?
- Describe the wedding in three words.

## Preserve the recordings

Download original audio files, keep a list of contributors if available and
make an independent backup. Create edited highlights from copies, not the only
masters.

Audio complements a [shared wedding photo
album](/en/shared-wedding-photo-album) by preserving voices and stories that
still images cannot.
