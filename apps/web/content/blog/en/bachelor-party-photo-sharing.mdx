Bachelor party photo sharing is easiest when the whole group receives one
tested link before the first activity. Pin it in the group chat, turn the same
link into a QR code at the accommodation and decide whether friends should
upload pictures from their phones or take new photos through a shared guest
camera.

That decision changes the experience. An upload album collects everything the
group chooses to send. A guest camera gives each person a limited roll and
makes taking the pictures part of the weekend. Neither is automatically better;
the right choice depends on whether you want a complete archive or a smaller,
more spontaneous record of the party.

## Choose the photo experience before the weekend

### Use an upload album for a complete camera-roll collection

An upload album works well when the group will use ordinary phone cameras,
record video or choose the best pictures later. It can also collect travel
photos taken before everyone meets and images from friends who join for only
one part of the itinerary.

Before picking a service, test the guest flow rather than relying on a feature
list. Check whether contributors need an account or app, whether iPhone and
Android users can both participate, whether video is accepted and who can see
or remove a contribution. Find out when uploads close and whether the organiser
can download the finished collection in one go.

### Use a guest camera when the limited roll is part of the fun

A browser-based guest camera is a different proposition. Friends scan or open
the link and take new pictures during the bachelor party. A finite roll makes
each shot a choice, while a later reveal can stop the group reviewing every
photo between activities.

With OurFilm, each guest opens the event in a phone browser, enters a name and
receives the roll selected by the host: 5, 10, 16, 24 or 36 frames. There is no
preview or retake. The private gallery can reveal immediately or when the event
ends.

If the host enables the optional after-event window, guests can add saved
photos for 24 hours after the event ends, using frames left on their original
rolls. It is not an unlimited camera-roll upload service and it does not accept
video. Choose a regular upload album instead if those are essential.

## Set one boundary for the whole itinerary

A bachelor or stag party may begin in a group chat, continue through a journey,
an activity and dinner, then finish at a rented house or late-night venue. One
album should cover that story without becoming another itinerary to manage.

Create the event before anyone travels and set its end after the last planned
activity, not after the first evening. Then give the same destination to every
member of the group:

- pin the direct link in the main chat;
- save the QR code to the organiser’s phone;
- print one card for the accommodation;
- keep a copy with the person leading the activities;
- add a short fallback URL if you print a sign.

Do not create separate albums for the journey, activity and night out unless
there is a genuine access reason. Splitting the weekend makes participation
harder and leaves the best pictures scattered again.

For a trip with several days, the same planning principles in the [group trip
photo-sharing guide](/en/blog/group-trip-photo-sharing) help with connectivity,
roles and the final archive.

## Give the best man a photo job, not every job

The best man or lead organiser is usually already tracking bookings, timings,
payments and people. Assigning one other friend as the photo lead prevents the
album from being forgotten when the schedule changes.

The photo lead should:

1. test the real guest link on two different phones;
2. remind the group once near the start;
3. keep the QR code available when people move venues;
4. help anyone whose browser lacks camera permission;
5. collect removal requests without turning them into a debate;
6. download and back up the finished album afterwards.

This person does not need to photograph everything. Their role is to keep the
shared route working so the whole group can contribute.

## Test the QR code where it will actually be used

A code that works on a laptop screen at home may be awkward on a glossy card in
a dim bar. Test the final size, contrast and destination before printing. Scan
it with both an iPhone and an Android phone, then complete the full camera or
upload flow as a guest.

Check the connection at the accommodation and important indoor venues. A QR
code can open only when the phone has a usable connection. If an activity takes
place underground, on the water or in an area with weak coverage, let people
use their normal cameras there and explain how those pictures will be collected
later. Do not promise an offline flow unless the chosen service genuinely has
one.

At a bar or restaurant, keep the code upright and away from drinks. At a rented
house, place it near the kitchen or common-room table rather than inside a
bedroom. When the group is moving around a city, the pinned link matters more
than a paper card left at the first stop.

## Tell friends whether to capture or upload

“Add your photos” is too vague when one product opens a camera and another asks
for existing files. Write the expected action and the reveal time beside the
code.

For an upload album:

> Add your bachelor weekend photos here. Upload your favourites before Sunday
> evening so the groom gets one complete album. No need to send them again in
> the chat.

For an OurFilm guest camera:

> Show the weekend from your point of view. Open the link, enter your name and
> use your 16 shots before Sunday at noon. There is no app, preview or retake.
> We will reveal the private gallery when the event ends.

Replace the frame count and deadline with the actual settings. If saved-photo
uploads are enabled for the following 24 hours, explain that they use whatever
frames remain; do not describe the window as unlimited catch-up storage.

## Plan a few pictures the groom will value later

The album does not need a complicated scavenger hunt, but a short optional list
helps friends look beyond repeated drinks-table photos. Useful prompts include:

- the full group before the first activity;
- the organiser during a moment they did not have to organise;
- a detail that identifies the destination;
- each stage of the journey from a different person’s seat;
- the groom with the friend who has known him longest;
- a candid conversation between activities;
- the scorecard, finished project or harmless result of the main activity;
- the group member normally behind the camera;
- one picture without matching outfits or props;
- the final breakfast, checkout or journey home.

If the group wants a game with teams and points, adapt the consent-friendly
structure in the [bachelorette photo challenge](/en/blog/bachelorette-photo-challenge)
rather than making strangers part of the joke.

## Keep the camera out of restricted or risky moments

Photography rules change from one stop to the next. Escape rooms may protect
their puzzles, performances may ban cameras, and activity providers may require
phones to stay in lockers. Ask before the weekend and remove any prompt that
conflicts with the venue’s rules.

Never make a photo worth more than someone’s safety. Phones stay away during
driving, cycling, water activities and any equipment briefing. A designated
spectator can photograph from an approved area, or that part of the schedule
can simply remain undocumented.

The same applies to strangers and staff. Do not turn bartenders, performers or
members of the public into compulsory challenge subjects. A memorable album
should come from the group’s own story, not from pressuring somebody outside
it to participate.

## Agree on privacy before the first night out

Bachelor-party pictures often include alcohol, accommodation details, travel
documents or jokes that make sense only inside the group. A private album is
not permission to publish its contents.

Set three plain rules before anyone contributes:

1. ask before photographing someone in a vulnerable or embarrassing moment;
2. send removal requests to the named photo lead without argument;
3. ask the people shown before reposting a picture publicly.

Do not photograph passports, booking references, room numbers, door codes or
payment details. If someone outside the group is recognisable, get their
agreement or choose another frame. Never photograph a person who cannot give
meaningful consent.

A QR code is a shareable link in visual form. Keep it in the invited group,
avoid posting it on a public story and remove venue cards before the group
leaves. For a more detailed access checklist, see the [private wedding album
guide](/en/blog/wedding-photo-album-privacy); the same principles apply to a
closed party.

## Finish the job after the party

Set a realistic deadline while everyone still knows what is expected. For an
upload album, one reminder on the journey home is usually more useful than a
series of messages weeks later. For a guest camera, make the reveal part of the
final breakfast or the first evening back, depending on the event end you chose.

The organiser should then review removal requests, download the complete
collection and create a separate backup. If the groom is not meant to see the
album until a gift or wedding speech, agree who holds the archive and when it
will be shared.

Do not rely on an event service as the only long-term copy. Keep the exported
files somewhere the intended recipient controls.

## Bachelor party photo-sharing checklist

- [ ] The group knows whether it should take new photos or upload existing ones.
- [ ] One album covers the real start and end of the itinerary.
- [ ] The final link works on iPhone, Android, Wi-Fi and mobile data.
- [ ] The link is pinned and the QR code travels with the organisers.
- [ ] Venue and activity photography rules are known.
- [ ] The frame count or upload deadline is written clearly.
- [ ] Everyone knows how to request removal and what may be posted publicly.
- [ ] Download and backup have a named owner.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to collect bachelor party photos?

Create one tested album or guest camera before the party and pin its link in
the main group chat. Use the same link as a QR code at the accommodation, state
whether friends should upload or take new pictures and appoint one person to
download the collection afterwards.

### Is this the same as stag party photo sharing?

Yes. “Bachelor party” is more common in North American English, while “stag
party” or “stag do” is common in the UK and Ireland. The practical setup is the
same: one contribution link, a clear event window and rules the whole group
understands.

### Do friends need an app to use OurFilm?

No. They open the event in a phone browser, enter a name and use the finite
roll chosen by the host. They do not need an account. Internet and camera
permission are required.

### Can the groom see the photos during the party?

The host chooses an immediate reveal or a reveal when the event ends. Use the
event-end option if the group wants the finished album to stay a surprise until
the weekend is over.

### Can friends upload all their phone photos after the bachelor party?

Not to OurFilm. If the host enables after-event uploads, guests have 24 hours
after the event ends to add saved photos using frames left on their original
rolls. It is not unlimited and it does not accept video; use an upload-album
service when you need a complete camera-roll collection.
