**In short:** a general shared album is best when guests already understand the
platform and the couple mainly wants storage. A dedicated wedding photo app is
best when the joining experience, printed QR code, moderation and event rules
need to work for a mixed guest list.

## What a shared album does well

Google Photos, iCloud and similar albums provide familiar browsing, comments
and long-term access inside an ecosystem the couple may already use. They can
be inexpensive and easy to keep after the wedding.

Their weakness is inconsistency. Guests may see different account, app and
permission prompts. Cross-platform contribution must be tested, not assumed.

## What a wedding-specific app adds

Wedding tools can provide:

- a printable QR code and guest-friendly landing page;
- uploads without a guest account;
- event branding and instructions;
- moderation and gallery privacy;
- video, guestbook or slideshow features;
- capture rules such as shot limits and reveal timing;
- a complete host download.

Not every wedding app includes every feature. Decide whether you want an upload
gallery or a shared camera before comparing products.

## Questions to ask before choosing

Test who can join, who can invite others, whether files are compressed, where
originals live, how long uploads remain open and what happens when storage
ends. Also test the actual QR code on both major phone platforms.

A [shared wedding photo album](/en/shared-wedding-photo-album) can remain the
final archive even if a dedicated event tool collects the media first.
