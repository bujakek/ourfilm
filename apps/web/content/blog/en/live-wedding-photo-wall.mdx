A live wedding photo wall displays guest uploads on a television, projector or
LED screen during the reception. It creates immediate participation, but it
needs moderation, reliable internet and a screen position that does not compete
with speeches or dancing.

## Choose the right source

Use a service with a purpose-built slideshow or photo-wall mode. Confirm whether
it supports video, captions, automatic rotation, moderation and an approval
queue. A browser tab that simply refreshes a folder is less reliable.

## Moderate before display

For a private family event, accidental uploads are more likely than malicious
ones, but both matter on a large screen. Assign a trusted person to approve or
hide images, especially before children, private messages or camera-roll files
appear publicly.

## Test the display path

Check the laptop, cable, adapter, power, screen resolution and browser before
guests arrive. Disable notifications, sleep mode and personal account pop-ups.
Use a neutral holding slide if the connection drops.

## Design for the screen

Landscape screens will crop portrait photos unless the slideshow adds borders
or fits the whole image. Choose a simple dark background, avoid tiny captions
and preview both orientations.

## Place it thoughtfully

The wall works well during drinks, dinner transitions and quieter dance-floor
periods. Dim or pause it during speeches, formal video and moments when the
screen would distract from real people.
