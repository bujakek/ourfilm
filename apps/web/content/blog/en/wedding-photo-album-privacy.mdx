**In short:** a wedding photo album is private only when access, contribution
and onward sharing are deliberately controlled. A hard-to-guess link helps, but
it does not stop an invited guest from forwarding that link or downloading a
photo.

## Set expectations before the event

Tell guests what the camera or upload link is for, who may see the gallery and
whether photos might later appear publicly. Give people a simple route to ask
for a photo to be hidden or removed.

Be especially careful with children, vulnerable guests and ceremonies where
photography is restricted. The couple's preference does not replace a parent's
or subject's reasonable privacy concern.

## Review access controls

Check whether the gallery is indexed, whether a password or PIN exists, who can
invite others, whether contributors can delete files and how long the link
remains active. Do not print a public social-media destination if the desired
outcome is a private family archive.

## Moderate before broader sharing

The host should be able to hide inappropriate, accidental or sensitive images.
Review the album before sending it to the entire guest list, and avoid facial
recognition or public tagging unless participants expect it.

## Download and close the loop

After the agreed contribution period, download the archive, make a separate
backup and close uploads that no longer need to remain open. Delete the online
event when its retention purpose ends.

These are practical design principles, not legal advice. For the guest flow,
compare a [private no-app photo-sharing
page](/en/wedding-photo-sharing-no-app) with your venue and jurisdiction's
requirements.
