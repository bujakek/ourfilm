Engagement party photo sharing works best when every guest has one clear place
to contribute and knows whether to upload existing pictures or take new ones.
For most parties, that means one tested QR code, a short instruction beside it
and an album that stays separate from the proposal and wedding collections.

That separation matters. The proposal may still be private, while the party is
often the first time relatives and friendship groups meet. A focused album
captures those introductions, toasts and unplanned reactions without turning
the evening into a rehearsal for the wedding.

## First decide which moment you are collecting

“Engagement photos” can describe three different sets of pictures:

- the proposal itself;
- a portrait session with the couple;
- the engagement party with family and friends.

Do not put all three behind the same public-facing instruction by default. A
surprise proposal may include images the couple has not announced yet. Portrait
session files usually belong with the photographer's delivery. Party pictures
are the collection guests can help create.

Name the album for the occasion, such as “Maya and Sam's engagement party,”
rather than simply “Our engagement.” Guests will immediately understand what
belongs there, and the couple can decide later whether any proposal or portrait
images should be copied into the finished collection.

If the party itself is a surprise announcement, keep the QR code off invitations
and public pages. Reveal it only after the couple has shared the news in the
room.

## Choose capture or upload before making the QR code

A QR code is only a shortcut to a web address. The experience after the scan is
the important part, so choose it before printing signs.

### Use an upload album for pictures guests already have

A conventional shared album is the practical choice when you want guests to
select photos from their camera rolls. It can collect proposal reactions,
travel pictures, polished group shots and contributions from people who upload
after getting home.

Check the real guest journey. Does it require an app, account or email address?
Can guests upload without seeing the rest of the album? How long does the link
accept new pictures? Test a full upload on both iPhone and Android rather than
stopping after the QR code opens.

### Use a guest camera for new pictures during the party

A digital guest camera invites people to photograph from inside the event. It
fits an engagement dinner, drinks party or backyard gathering when candid
pictures are more important than collecting every image already stored on
everyone's phone.

With OurFilm, guests scan the event QR code, enter a name and receive a finite

That creates a different behaviour from an upload folder: guests decide what
deserves a frame, then return to the party instead of reviewing a camera roll.
It also means OurFilm is not the right tool when your main goal is unlimited
uploads of existing photos.

## Give the party its own album

It is tempting to create the wedding album months early and use it for every
celebration. A separate engagement-party collection is usually easier to
explain and manage.

- The guest list may not match the wedding guest list.
- The couple may not have chosen a wedding date or photo plan yet.
- Proposal pictures may need a smaller audience.
- A venue, planner or photographer may have different permissions at the
  wedding.
- The engagement album can be closed and backed up without leaving a link open
  for months.

When the wedding plan is ready, create a new destination and a new QR code. If
you are planning that larger collection now, use the separate
[wedding photo-sharing guide](/en/blog/wedding-photo-sharing-as-a-shared-film)
to decide who contributes, when it opens and how it will be preserved.

## Plan for the moments a hired photographer may miss

The engagement party album should complement professional coverage, not try to
replace it. If a photographer is present for one or two hours, ask guests to
notice the viewpoints that do not need staging:

- the first hug between relatives meeting for the first time;
- friends arriving while the couple is greeting someone else;
- the person listening during a toast, not only the speaker;
- the ring being shown at a table;
- handwritten cards, family recipes or small details with a story;
- the kitchen, garden or balcony between formal moments;
- the last relaxed conversation after the planned programme ends.

Keep the prompts optional. Six useful ideas on a table card are better than a
long scavenger hunt that makes every guest chase the same photograph.

If the party has several groups who do not know one another, use prompts that
create introductions: “someone you met tonight,” “the two families together”
or “the friend who knows the couple's oldest story.” Avoid competitive tasks
that interrupt dinner or pressure people who prefer not to be photographed.

## Fit the QR code into the party timeline

The code needs different treatment at an intimate dinner and a larger drinks
party. In both cases, introduce it after guests have settled in rather than
expecting an entrance sign to do all the work.

### Before guests arrive

Create the final event, open it from a phone that is not signed in as the host
and complete the exact action a guest will take. Test the smallest printed code
in normal room lighting. If the venue has unreliable Wi-Fi, repeat the test on
mobile data.

Choose one person to own the album. That person should know the reveal timing,
the download plan and what to do if someone asks for a picture to be hidden.

### During arrivals

Place one sign near the welcome drinks, coat area or seating plan, but do not
make guests scan while they are holding gifts and greeting the couple. A small
card on each table lets them return to the activity later.

### Before the first toast

Ask the host to give one short explanation:

> We made a guest camera for tonight. Scan the code on your table, enter your
> name and photograph the moments we cannot see. You have 16 frames, and the
> album will reveal after the party.

Change the frame count and reveal wording to match the settings you actually
selected. If you use an upload album instead, say “choose and upload your
photos”; do not tell guests to take pictures inside it.

### After the final planned moment

Put a last reminder near the exit or in the event group chat, but send the same
link—not a second album. With OurFilm, the host can optionally allow guests to
select saved photos during the 24 hours after the event. Those additions use
the remaining frames from each guest's original roll, and video is not
accepted. State that limit clearly if you enable the window.

## Use wording that tells guests exactly what happens

The best QR sign answers four questions in a few lines:

1. What is this?
2. What should I do?
3. Do I need an app or account?
4. When can I see the pictures?

For an upload album:

> Add your engagement-party photos. Scan the code, choose your favourites and
> upload them to our shared album before Sunday evening.

For an OurFilm guest camera:

> Photograph the engagement party from your seat. Scan, enter your name and
> use your 16 frames tonight. No app or account. The album reveals after the
> party.

For a surprise announcement:

> Now that the secret is out, help us remember everyone's reaction. Scan the
> code and capture the celebration from your point of view.

Do not advertise “unlimited photos,” “live slideshow” or “video” for a setup
that does not provide those things. A precise instruction produces fewer
support questions than a clever headline.

## Place the code where people can act on it

Good placements follow natural pauses:

- a welcome-drinks table after the entrance queue;
- table numbers, menus or place cards;
- beside the cake, dessert or champagne station;
- near a guest book, card box or photo backdrop;
- on a stationary screen before speeches;
- a small sign beside the exit for the final reminder.

Use a dark code on a light, matte background with empty space around it. Avoid
mirror acrylic, curved bottles, folds and places where candles or spotlights
create glare. Add a readable fallback link below the code and make both routes
open the same event.

For a house party, two or three copies are usually enough. For a venue with
separate rooms or a garden, repeat the same code in each active area. More
destinations create confusion; more copies of one destination create access.

## Protect the announcement and the people in the pictures

A QR code is a shareable link, not a password. Anyone who receives or
photographs it may be able to open the destination, so keep it within the
invited group and explain who will be able to see the resulting gallery.

Agree on the public-announcement plan before the party. If some relatives have
not heard the news, ask guests not to post until a named time. A contribution
to the party album is not automatic permission to publish the picture on a
public social account.

Also decide how to handle:

- children and relatives who do not want to appear online;
- close-ups of cards, addresses or gifts with personal information;
- pictures of someone eating, drinking or caught in an awkward moment;
- proposal images that one partner has not approved for wider sharing;
- requests to hide or remove a photograph.

OurFilm lets the host hide or delete pictures from the event gallery. Whatever
service you choose, name one person who will receive removal requests and
review the collection before anything is posted publicly. The more detailed
[wedding album privacy guide](/en/blog/wedding-photo-album-privacy) also
applies to engagement celebrations.

## Engagement-party photo-sharing checklist

- [ ] Proposal, portrait and party pictures have separate destinations where
      needed.
- [ ] The guest action is clearly capture or upload.
- [ ] The final QR code works on iPhone, Android, Wi-Fi and mobile data.
- [ ] The sign states app requirements, contribution deadline and reveal time.
- [ ] The same code appears at two or three useful pause points.
- [ ] One host will explain the activity before the first toast.
- [ ] Guests know whether the engagement is safe to post publicly.
- [ ] One person owns moderation, download and backup.
- [ ] The wedding will receive a new album and code when its plan is ready.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to collect engagement-party photos?

Use one shared destination, connect it to one tested QR code and put a direct
instruction beside it. Choose an upload album for existing camera-roll photos
or a guest camera for new pictures taken during the celebration.

### Should the engagement party and wedding use the same photo album?

Usually not. The guest lists, permissions, timing and photo activities may be
different. A separate engagement-party album is easier to close and back up,
while the wedding can have its own QR code and instructions later.

### Where should I put an engagement-party photo QR code?

Place it where guests pause with a free hand: on tables, by welcome drinks,
near the cake or guest book and beside the exit. Repeat the same code rather
than creating several competing albums.

### Can guests upload existing photos to OurFilm?

Only if the host enables the optional after-event upload window. For 24 hours
after the event ends, guests can use the frames left in their original roll to
select saved photos. It is not an unlimited upload folder, and it does not
accept video.

### Is an engagement-party QR album private?

It can be kept within the invited group, but the QR code itself is a shareable
link. Do not post it publicly, tell guests who can see the gallery and assign
someone to handle hide or removal requests.
