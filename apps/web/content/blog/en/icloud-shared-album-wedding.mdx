**In short:** an iCloud Shared Album can be convenient for an Apple-heavy
wedding group. It is less dependable when the guest list mixes iPhone, Android
and people who do not use an Apple account.

## The important compatibility test

Do not test only on the couple's signed-in iPhones. Invite another Apple user,
then open the public view on an Android phone and a desktop browser. Confirm who
can merely view the album and who can actually contribute.

Public web viewing does not automatically provide the same upload experience as
joining through Apple's ecosystem. If every guest should add photos, document
the steps they will see rather than writing “scan and upload” on the sign.

## Permissions and privacy

Decide whether subscribers may post, comment or invite others. A public website
is easier to view across devices but also broadens link-based access. Keep
sensitive family photos in a more controlled space and close contribution when
the collection period ends.

## Quality and backup

Shared albums are designed for convenient viewing, not as the only archive of
print-ready originals. Preserve the original camera files separately and test a
download before promising full-resolution delivery.

Choose iCloud when the group is small and Apple-based. Choose a cross-platform
[shared wedding photo album](/en/shared-wedding-photo-album) when every guest
needs the same browser flow, or a digital camera experience when participation
during the event matters more than existing uploads.
