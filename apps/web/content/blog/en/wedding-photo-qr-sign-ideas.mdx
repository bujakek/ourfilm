A good wedding photo QR sign explains the reward before asking for the scan.
“Help us capture the moments we miss” is clearer than a code with no context.
Add the action, any shot limit and the reveal time in a few lines.

## Five useful sign formats

1. **Table card:** small, repeated and easy to scan while seated.
2. **Welcome sign insert:** creates awareness at arrival but needs later repeats.
3. **Guestbook frame:** connects photo and written contributions.
4. **Bar sign:** catches guests during a natural pause.
5. **Photo-corner card:** appears exactly where the activity happens.

## Design rules

Keep dark QR modules on a plain light area with generous blank space. Do not
place the code over a photograph, metallic texture or patterned background.
Use the wedding typeface for headings, not inside the code itself.

Matte print usually scans more reliably than a glossy surface under venue
lights. Add a short fallback URL beneath the code and keep instructions large
enough to read in dim light.

## Copy structure

Use three levels:

- a benefit-led heading;
- one-sentence instruction;
- practical detail such as “24 photos each” or “reveals after midnight.”

Avoid claiming “no app” unless the complete guest journey has actually been
tested without one.

Print one production sample and scan it from the real distance on multiple
phones. The [wedding QR-code setup
guide](/en/qr-code-for-wedding-photos) covers the destination behind the sign.
