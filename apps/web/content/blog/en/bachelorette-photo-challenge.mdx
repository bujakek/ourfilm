A bachelorette photo challenge works best when every prompt creates a picture
the bride will actually want after the party. Choose 10 to 15 tasks for a
single evening or use all 30 across a weekend, make every challenge optional
and give the group one clear place to capture or collect the results.

The list below avoids embarrassing strangers, forced drinking and dares that
turn the bride into a prop. It is designed for a dinner, house weekend, city
break or night out, including groups that do not drink.

## 30 bachelorette photo challenge prompts

Mix easy group pictures with a few creative tasks. Do not require the prompts
to be completed in order.

### Start with the group

1. **The before photo:** everyone together before coats, weather and dancing
   change the plan.
2. **The bride's first laugh:** a candid picture from the first hour.
3. **One matching detail:** shoes, bracelets, sunglasses or another part of the
   theme.
4. **The view from your seat:** show the party from one guest's perspective.
5. **The theme colour:** find it somewhere unexpected and include a member of
   the group.
6. **Hands in:** photograph rings, nails, wristbands or friendship bracelets
   without needing everyone's face.

### Tell the friendship story

7. **Then and now:** recreate an old friendship photo.
8. **Longest friendship:** the bride with the person who has known her longest.
9. **Newest friendship:** the bride with the newest member of the group.
10. **Signature pose:** everyone copies the bride's most recognisable pose.
11. **An inside joke:** stage it so the group will understand it years later.
12. **The unofficial photographer:** capture the person usually taking every
    picture.

### Use the location

13. **A local landmark:** the group and one detail that identifies the place.
14. **A reflection:** use a mirror, window or puddle without blocking a public
    walkway.
15. **The journey:** a safe moment on the train, in a taxi queue or walking to
    the next stop.
16. **The menu choice:** the dish, snack or alcohol-free drink the group will
    remember.
17. **From above:** a table, picnic or collection of party details.
18. **The weather save:** show how the group handled rain, wind or unexpected
    heat.

### Capture the party, not just poses

19. **The first toast:** glasses can contain anything.
20. **The best dance move:** photograph it only where cameras are permitted.
21. **Everyone singing:** catch the expression rather than asking for a video.
22. **A quiet minute:** the bride between activities, not looking at the camera.
23. **The organiser relaxing:** proof that the planner joined the party too.
24. **A detail the bride missed:** decoration, note or tiny moment happening
    behind her.

### Finish with keepsakes

25. **Pass the camera:** each person photographs the next member of the group.
26. **Two generations:** include a parent, relative or older friend if they are
    part of the celebration.
27. **Advice without words:** act out one piece of marriage advice in a frame.
28. **Save it for the reveal:** take one picture the bride should not see until
    the collection opens.
29. **The honest group photo:** no arranged pose; everyone stands exactly as
    they are.
30. **The ending:** the group at the last planned stop or back at the house.

## Pick a format that suits the group

The same prompt list can feel relaxed or competitive depending on the rules.

### One shared checklist

Put 10 to 15 tasks in the group chat. Anyone can complete any prompt, and more
than one interpretation is welcome. This is the easiest format for a small
group moving between several places.

### Team challenge

Split into pairs or trios and give each team the same list. Award one point per
completed prompt and one bonus point for the bride's favourite interpretation.
Keep the scoring secondary; the useful result is a varied set of pictures.

### Photo bingo

Place 16 prompts in a four-by-four grid. A row wins, but the group can continue
until the end of the evening. Bingo works well at a house party because nobody
has to leave the venue to find a task.

### Limited-roll version

Give each person a fixed number of shots and no chance to retake them. This
changes the game from “finish everything” to “choose what matters.” Assigning
different prompts to different guests prevents everyone spending a frame on
the same toast.

## Decide how the photos will come together

Choose the collection method before you design cards or send the itinerary.

**Standard phone cameras plus an upload album** suit a challenge where every
task must be reviewed, retaken or uploaded after the weekend. Check whether the
album requires an account, whether everyone can contribute and whether the
group can download the originals.

**A browser-based guest camera** suits a challenge where uncertainty is part
of the fun. With OurFilm, guests open the event from one link or QR code, enter
a name and take new photos with a finite roll. There is no preview or retake;
the private gallery can open immediately or when the event ends. OurFilm does
not import saved photos or accept video, so choose a normal upload album if
either is essential.

For a multi-day trip, make sure the capture window covers the planned
activities. Internet is required to use the camera. If part of the itinerary
has unreliable coverage, use ordinary phone cameras there and choose a
collection method that accepts those saved pictures later.

## Set the rules without draining the fun

Keep the complete rules on one screen or small card:

> Pick any 12 photo prompts before midnight. Every task is optional. Ask before
> photographing anyone outside our group, follow the venue's rules and do not
> post another person's picture without checking. We will open the collection
> together tomorrow.

Useful boundaries are simple:

- no entering staff-only, dangerous or restricted areas;
- no photographing strangers as the joke;
- no task that requires alcohol;
- no public posting without the people in the picture agreeing;
- no points for making someone uncomfortable;
- one designated organiser can stop or replace any prompt.

If the bride dislikes competitive games, remove points entirely. Give everyone
three prompts and let the finished collection reveal how differently they saw
the same event.

## Share the challenge at the right moment

Send practical information before the party, but keep the prompts until the
group is together if you want surprise. Pin the link in the group chat and put
the QR code at the accommodation, dinner table or getting-ready area.

Explain the game once before people split up:

> We have one shared photo challenge for the weekend. Scan the code, choose the
> prompts you like and capture the party from your point of view. You do not
> need an app, and we will see the pictures together when the event ends.

For a venue-based party, check its photography policy. A club, show, spa or
workshop may restrict cameras even when personal phone photos are normally
allowed elsewhere.

## Keep the final collection private

The group may be comfortable taking a picture without wanting it on a public
feed. Treat the shared link as an invitation, tell everyone who can view the
gallery and give them a simple way to request that a photo be hidden or
removed.

Review the collection before posting a recap. A person's participation in the
challenge is not blanket permission to publish every picture of them. Be
especially careful with venue staff, people outside the group and location
details that reveal where the group is staying.

## Bachelorette photo challenge checklist

- [ ] The bride is comfortable with the game.
- [ ] The list fits the schedule and locations.
- [ ] Every prompt can be skipped without a penalty.
- [ ] The capture or upload method has been tested on two phones.
- [ ] The event window covers the whole planned challenge.
- [ ] The link is pinned and the QR code is easy to find.
- [ ] Consent, venue and posting rules are clear.
- [ ] Someone owns the final review, download and backup.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many bachelorette photo challenge prompts should we use?

Use 10 to 15 for one evening and up to 30 for a full weekend. A shorter list
with optional tasks usually produces better pictures than a long checklist
everyone rushes to finish.

### Should a bachelorette photo challenge have teams?

Teams work for a competitive group, while one shared checklist is easier when
people move between venues. Pairs are a useful middle ground because nobody
has to complete a task alone.

### What is the difference between a bachelorette scavenger hunt and a photo challenge?

A scavenger hunt may involve finding objects, places or answers. A photo
challenge requires a picture for each prompt and leaves the group with a visual
record of the celebration.

### Can we run the photo challenge without an app?

Yes. Use a browser-based guest camera or an upload page that opens from a link
or QR code. Test the whole contribution flow before the event rather than
assuming “no app” also means no account or permission steps.

### Can guests upload existing pictures to OurFilm?

No. OurFilm is for taking new photos during the event. If the group needs to
select saved pictures afterwards or contribute videos, use an upload album for
this challenge.
