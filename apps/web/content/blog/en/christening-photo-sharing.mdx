Christening photo sharing works best when the family receives one tested link
and one clear instruction before the celebration. Use the same destination as
a QR code at the meal or reception, name one person to manage it and decide
whether relatives should upload existing pictures or take new ones through a
shared guest camera.

The ceremony needs a different plan from the gathering afterwards. Follow the
church or venue's photography rules, leave the formal coverage to the agreed
photographer and invite guests to contribute once they can do so without
interrupting the service.

## Decide whether relatives should upload or take photos

### Choose an upload album for a complete collection

A conventional upload album is useful when relatives will photograph with
their normal camera apps and choose their favourites later. It can collect
professional previews, phone pictures from the journey, portraits taken before
the service and video, if the selected service supports it.

Test the contributor experience before choosing a provider. Check whether a
guest needs an account or app, whether both iPhone and Android work, whether
video is accepted and who can remove an accidental upload. Confirm how long
contributions stay open and how the organiser downloads the finished album.

### Choose a guest camera when taking the picture is the activity

A browser-based guest camera is a different experience. Each relative opens
the camera and takes new photographs during the event instead of searching a
phone library. A limited roll encourages people to notice a few meaningful
moments rather than send every duplicate.

With OurFilm, a guest scans the QR code or opens the link, enters a name and
receives the roll chosen by the host: 5, 10, 16, 24 or 36 frames. There is no
app or guest account, and there is no preview or retake. The host can reveal
the private gallery immediately or when the event ends.

If the host enables saved-photo uploads, guests have 24 hours after the event
ends to add photographs already on their phones, using frames left on their
original rolls. This is not an unlimited upload album and it does not accept
video. Use a dedicated upload service if a complete camera-roll archive is the
priority.

## Separate the ceremony from the celebration

A baptism or christening often has two very different settings: a quiet
service and a family meal, garden gathering or reception. Do not treat both as
one continuous photo challenge.

Ask the church, officiant or venue what is allowed before printing any
instruction. Flash, movement near the font or photography during particular
parts of the service may be restricted. If a professional or nominated family
member is covering the ceremony, tell everyone else when to keep phones away.

The shared camera becomes most useful afterwards. At the reception, guests can
record the details and conversations the official photographer may not see:
the godparents greeting relatives, grandparents meeting across the table, the
cake before it is cut and children playing between family groups.

If there is no reception, share the direct link privately after the service and
state exactly what belongs in the album. A QR code is helpful only when people
have a natural place and enough time to use it.

## Give the album one owner

The parents may create the album, but they should not have to troubleshoot
phones while caring for the child and greeting every relative. A godparent,
sibling or close friend can act as the photo lead.

That person should:

1. test the final guest route on an iPhone and an Android phone;
2. confirm the ceremony's photography rules;
3. place and remove the QR signs at the agreed times;
4. help older relatives open the link and allow camera access;
5. receive requests to hide or delete a picture;
6. download and back up the finished collection.

The role is practical, not editorial. The photo lead does not need to approve
every frame or persuade reluctant guests to appear. They keep the shared route
working and respect a simple no.

## Keep photographs of children inside the intended circle

At a christening, the subject is usually a child who cannot decide where a
picture is published. Other children may also be present. The parents or
guardians should set the sharing boundary before the QR code is distributed.

Agree on three separate actions:

- taking a photograph;
- adding it to the family album;
- reposting it on a public social account.

Permission for one does not automatically cover the others. Tell contributors
not to publish from the album without checking with the parents and the people
shown. Avoid photographs that expose certificates, addresses, school or
nursery details, car registrations or travel documents.

A QR code is a shareable link in visual form, not a password. Keep it off
public posts, collect printed cards when the gathering ends and do not include
it in photographs uploaded publicly. The [wedding album privacy
guide](/en/blog/wedding-photo-album-privacy) has a fuller access checklist that
also applies to a closed family event.

## Put the QR code where phones are welcome

One clear sign at the meal is more useful than several signs around a church.
Place it upright near the guest book, seating plan, cake table or entrance to
the reception room. If the family sits at several tables, add a small card to
each table without covering place names or menus.

The printed instruction should say what happens after the scan. For an upload
album, write something like:

> Add your favourite christening photos to our private family album before
> Sunday evening. Please ask us before sharing a picture publicly.

For an OurFilm guest camera:

> Show the celebration from your point of view. Scan, enter your name and use
> your 10-picture roll before 5 pm. There is no preview or retake. We will open
> the family gallery when the event ends.

Replace the example frame count and time with the actual settings. Add a short
typed link below the code for a guest whose camera will not scan it, and keep
the direct link in the family message thread as a backup.

## Make the flow comfortable for every generation

A no-app browser flow removes one barrier, but it does not make every phone or
every guest identical. Test the actual route in advance and expect to help a
few relatives with the camera permission prompt, mobile data or text size.

At the gathering, let the photo lead demonstrate the full flow on their own
phone rather than taking someone else's device. A relative without a suitable
phone can ask a trusted family member to take a photograph from their point of
view. Nobody should need to create a new email account or hand over a password
just to join an event album.

Keep the instructions short. “Scan, enter your name, take a photo” is easier
to follow than a feature list. If the venue has weak reception, provide its
guest Wi-Fi details where appropriate and test them before the family arrives.
A QR code cannot open without a working connection.

## Plan the photographs that connect the generations

The most valuable christening album is not necessarily the one with the most
pictures. A short optional prompt list can make sure the collection includes
people and details the parents may miss.

Useful prompts include:

- the child with each godparent;
- the child with grandparents from each side of the family;
- three generations in one frame;
- the full family group outside the church or at the reception;
- the invitation, candle, clothing or another meaningful detail;
- relatives arriving and greeting one another;
- the table before the meal begins;
- the cake before and after the first slice;
- the person who organised the day;
- a quiet conversation away from the formal group photographs.

Do not make the service itself a scavenger hunt. Formal group portraits are
best assigned to one confident person with a simple list, while candid prompts
can begin once the ceremony is over.

For a smaller child-centred gathering, the practical setup in the [baby shower
photo-sharing guide](/en/blog/baby-shower-photo-sharing) can also help with QR
placement, assistance and a calm pace.

## Set an event end that matches the real day

Do not close the camera when the service finishes if the family is continuing
to lunch. Include the reception, gift opening and farewells, then state the
real closing time beside the QR code.

An immediate reveal works when relatives want to browse together during the
meal. An event-end reveal keeps attention on the people in the room and gives
the family one album-opening moment later. Neither choice changes who should
be allowed to receive the link.

After the gathering, deal with removal requests, download the full collection
and save a second copy somewhere controlled by the family. Do not rely on an
event service as the only long-term archive. If the parents plan a printed
book, wait until they have reviewed the private collection before sending files
to a printer or designer.

## Christening photo-sharing checklist

- [ ] The family has chosen an upload album or a guest camera.
- [ ] The church or venue's photography rules are confirmed.
- [ ] One person owns the QR signs, guest help and final download.
- [ ] The link and complete guest flow work on iPhone and Android.
- [ ] Parents have stated what may enter the album and what may be public.
- [ ] The code appears where phone use is welcome, not during the service.
- [ ] The frame count, closing time and reveal plan are written clearly.
- [ ] Formal group photographs have a short, named shot list.
- [ ] The finished album will be downloaded and backed up.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to collect christening photos?

Create one album or guest camera before the day, test its real contribution
flow and share one link with the invited family. Display the same link as a QR
code at the meal or reception, explain whether guests should upload or take
new pictures, and appoint one person to download the collection afterwards.

### Is christening photo sharing the same as baptism photo sharing?

The event names vary by church, family and region, but the collection problem
is the same. Confirm the service's photography rules, then use one private
family route for the celebration pictures. Use the wording that appears on the
family's invitation rather than forcing a different label.

### Do relatives need an app to use OurFilm?

No. A guest opens the event in a phone browser, enters a name and uses the
finite roll chosen by the host. No guest account is required. A working
internet connection and camera permission are still needed.

### Can guests see the photos during the reception?

The host chooses whether the gallery reveals immediately or when the event
ends. An immediate reveal supports browsing together; an event-end reveal
keeps the finished collection hidden while people are still taking pictures.

### Can guests add saved phone photos after the christening?

Only if the host enables the optional after-event window. It remains open for
24 hours after the event ends, uses frames left on each guest's original roll
and accepts photos rather than video. It is not an unlimited upload album.
