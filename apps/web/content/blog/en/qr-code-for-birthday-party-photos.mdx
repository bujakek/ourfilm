A QR code for birthday party photos gives every guest the same place to
contribute. Before you print it, decide what should happen after the scan:
should guests upload pictures already on their phones, or take new pictures in
a shared guest camera? That one choice changes the instructions, timing and
kind of album you will get.

For a birthday, the simplest plan is one tested destination, a short sentence
beside the code and copies placed where people actually take photos. The QR code
is only the doorway; the experience behind it does the real work.

## Choose between an upload album and a guest camera

Both options gather pictures in one place, but they solve different problems.

### Use an upload album when you want existing photos

An upload album lets guests select images from their camera roll. It is useful
when people may contribute pictures taken before the party, upload several
versions of a group shot or add photos after they get home.

Check whether the service needs an app or account, whether guests can see one
another's uploads and how long contributions remain open. Test the exact upload
flow rather than assuming every browser behaves the same way.

### Use a guest camera when the activity is part of the party

A browser-based guest camera asks people to scan and take new photos during the
event. A finite roll can make each picture feel intentional instead of turning
the night into an unlimited upload task.

OurFilm follows this model. Each guest opens the camera in a browser, enters a
name and receives the number of frames chosen by the host: 5, 10, 16, 24 or 36.
There is no preview or retake, and the gallery can reveal immediately or when
the event ends. It does not accept video or audio. If the host enables
after-event uploads, guests can add saved photos only during the 24 hours
after the event ends, using remaining frames in their original roll.

## Match the setup to the birthday

The right instructions depend more on the party than on the age printed on the
cake.

### Milestone birthday

At a 30th, 40th, 50th or 60th birthday, guests often arrive from different
parts of the host's life. Give them prompts that connect those groups: “Find
someone who knew me at school,” “Recreate an old photo,” or “Capture the best
toast.” Put the code near the bar and tables as well as the entrance.

### Surprise party

Do not send the public-facing QR code in a message the guest of honour might
see. Brief the organisers separately, display the code after the reveal and
choose a gallery opening time that preserves the surprise.

### Kids' birthday

Make the instructions for parents and supervising adults, not for children to
navigate alone. Decide before the event who may view or download the pictures,
and respect any parent or guardian who does not want their child photographed.
Keep participation optional.

### Dinner or house party

One or two visible placements may be enough in a small room. A sign by the cake
captures the ceremonial moments; a second by the drinks or music catches the
rest of the night. At a larger venue, repeat the same code rather than creating
separate albums for each area.

## Set it up before anything goes to print

Use this order so the printed code points to the final destination.

1. **Choose the contribution method.** Decide between saved-photo uploads and
   new-photo capture.
2. **Create the real event.** Set the event name, opening and closing time,
   guest access and reveal behaviour.
3. **Copy the final link.** Generate the QR code only after the destination is
   complete.
4. **Scan it on real phones.** Test an iPhone and Android device from the scan
   through permission prompts and one completed contribution.
5. **Try the venue connection.** Test both guest Wi-Fi and mobile data where
   the signs will stand.
6. **Print one physical sample.** Check it at normal viewing distance and in
   the room's evening light before ordering more.

If the service includes a capture window, open it early enough for setup and
late arrivals. Do not rely on a code that has only been tested from the host's
logged-in account.

## Write instructions people can act on

“Scan me” explains the mechanism but not the reason. A useful sign answers
three questions: what should I do, do I need an app, and when will I see the
photos?

For a shared upload album:

> Add your birthday photos. Scan the code, choose your favourites and upload
> them to our private album. No group chat needed.

For a digital guest camera:

> Help us photograph the party. Scan the code, enter your name and use your 16
> shots before the night ends. No app or account. The album opens tomorrow.

For a playful milestone party:

> Show us the birthday from your table. Scan, take a photo and save a frame for
> the dance floor. We will reveal the roll after the party.

Replace the frame count and reveal timing with the settings you actually chose.
A fallback short link can help when a camera cannot read the printed code, but
it should lead to the same destination.

## Put the QR code where photos happen

An entrance sign is useful, but guests are busy greeting people when they
arrive. Repeat the code at natural pauses:

- beside the cake or dessert table;
- on dining tables or menu cards;
- at the bar or drinks station;
- near a photo backdrop or prop table;
- at the edge of the dance floor, away from spills;
- on a screen during speeches, if the code remains large and still.

Use dark code modules on a light, matte background and leave clear space around
the square. Avoid folds, curved balloons and glossy reflections. The smallest
printed version matters most, so test that version instead of only scanning a
large code from a laptop screen.

## Give guests a reason to join in

One spoken reminder usually works better than adding more signs. Ask the host,
DJ or organiser to explain the activity after most guests have arrived:

> There is a birthday camera on your table. Scan the code and capture the
> moments I cannot see. You have 16 frames, and we will open the album tomorrow.

Then offer a short prompt list rather than a rigid scavenger hunt:

- the first slice of cake;
- three generations in one frame;
- the loudest laugh;
- an old friend and a new friend;
- the view from your seat;
- the last people dancing.

Prompts help quieter tables start, while leaving room for the unplanned images
that make a guest album worth keeping.

## Protect privacy without overpromising it

A QR code is a visual form of a link, not a password. Anyone who receives or
photographs it may be able to open the destination. Keep the code within the
invited group, check the service's access controls and explain who will see the
finished gallery.

Name one person guests can contact if they want a picture hidden or removed.
For children's parties, agree on photography expectations with parents or
guardians before sharing the album more widely. Review the pictures before
posting any of them publicly.

After the party, download the collection, keep a separate backup and close the
contribution window when it is no longer needed. Do not treat a guest's private
contribution as permission to use their image in public or commercial posts.

## Birthday photo QR-code checklist

- [ ] The guest action is clearly either capture or upload.
- [ ] The final event link is behind the printed QR code.
- [ ] iPhone, Android, Wi-Fi and mobile-data tests are complete.
- [ ] The sign states app requirements and reveal timing.
- [ ] The same code appears at two or more natural photo points.
- [ ] A host or organiser will announce it once.
- [ ] Child-photo and wider-sharing expectations are clear.
- [ ] Download, backup and link closure have an owner.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I collect birthday party photos with a QR code?

Create one shared camera or upload album, connect its final URL to a QR code,
test the whole guest journey and place copies where people pause and take
photos. Add one sentence explaining what to do and when the album opens.

### Do birthday guests need to download an app?

Not always. Many services, including OurFilm, use a browser-based guest flow.
Check the exact service before printing and state “no app” only if your tested
setup really works that way.

### Can guests upload photos already on their phones to OurFilm?

Only if the host enables after-event uploads. Guests can select saved photos
only during the 24 hours after the event ends, using the remaining frames in
their original roll. Videos are not accepted.

### Where should I display a birthday photo QR code?

Use the entrance as an introduction, then repeat the same code near the cake,
tables, drinks or photo area. Place it where guests have a free hand and enough
light to scan.

### Is a QR-code birthday album private?

It can be limited to invited guests, but the QR code itself is not a password.
Review access settings, avoid public posting of the code and tell guests who
can view or download the finished pictures.
